# Dig Through Worlds

A browser digging game in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and no npm dependencies. The world is a 7×7 column of ground, 50 layers deep, drawn as an isometric pit. A kid miner clears the pit one layer at a time, walking to the nearest block and digging it on its own; the player can steer to pick the next block and taps to dig faster. Ore blocks give five materials (coal, copper, iron, gold, crystal) that pay for upgrades alongside coins, early worlds giving the low tiers and later worlds the high ones; fossils and treasure chests fill a museum with three skeletons per world (dinosaurs, a mammoth, sea reptiles and a few alien beasts), the first buried near the top so finds come early. On the surface the miner walks about a plaza: the pit, a shop stall, a helpers' hut, a world signpost and a museum hall whose plinths show each world's skeleton as its fossils are found, with visitors wandering between them. Each of the five worlds is unlocked by bringing home the previous world's relic.

## Layout

- `index.html` is the page shell. It loads the scripts below with plain `<script>` tags, in this order, so the game also runs from `file://`. The stylesheet and scripts carry a `?v=N` stamp: bump it on every deploy, because GitHub Pages lets browsers keep each file for 10 minutes and a phone could otherwise run new HTML with old scripts.
- `css/style.css` holds all styles. The HUD is one row above the pit (the plaza shows coins, museum income and a large gold ☰ menu button; the pit shows coins, depth and bag), and small Home and Auto buttons float over the bottom-left of the pit on every screen; in the pit a round ⛏ Faster button sits bottom-right (each press is one `DTW.tap`), and a full bag turns Home into a pulsing gold "🎒 Full! ⬆ Home" button. Laptop and desktop screens (wide, at least 1100×640) scale the HUD and these buttons up. HUD chips are styled as `#hud .chip`, since `.chip` alone is the shop's price chip. A joystick (`#stick`) is hidden until a finger is held or dragged on the scene (`HOLD` 0.18 s or `DRAG` 12 px in `ui.js`); it is rooted where the finger went down and hides when it lifts. Portrait screens show the station menu as a sheet lifted above the Dig button; wide screens (landscape, at least 700 px) fill the window with the pit, float the HUD over it too, and show the station menu as a right-hand panel. A station's menu opens while the miner stands on its pad; the ☰ button (or Esc) opens a menu anywhere on the plaza that reaches every station (Shop first) and the sound switch, with small Restart world and Start over buttons at the bottom that act only after the player types yes. `ui.js` tells the renderer how wide that panel is (`DTWRender.setView`) so the camera centres the pit in the uncovered part.
- `js/logic.js` is the pure game logic: the world table, terrain generation, economy, unlock rules, the plaza map and walking (`HUB`, `hubInput`, `hubWalkTo`, `hubStation`) and save/load. It has no DOM access and defines the global `DTW`.
- `js/post.js` is the HD-2D post-process (`DTWPost`): a WebGL2 pass with no libraries that lights the scene with screen-space point lights, blurs everything outside the working layer (tilt-shift depth of field), adds bloom from an emissive layer and grades the colours per world.
- `js/sprites.js` is the pixel art for characters and exhibits (`DTWSprites`). Characters are built from parts written as letter grids (heads, torso, legs), recoloured by palettes (the miner, visitors, guide, shopkeeper), posed and outlined; the miner's rucksack grows in five steps as its bag fills (`pack` is the fill fraction), and a fossil being carried home rides on its back (`bone`). Legs and arms are drawn per frame from one shared walk cycle (`walkPose`: two steps of `STEP_X` sprite pixels, so `render.js` advances it by cells walked and feet do not skate) and one shared two-handed pickaxe swing (`swing`, striking at phase 0.72); the miner, helpers and plaza people all use them. The swing is key poses in `SWING_SIDE` and `SWING_DOWN` after HD-2D sword swings: the hands move on an arc about the shoulders and the handle turns about the grip; the pick is lifted, held cocked behind the hat, struck in two quick frames with a smear, and held low in a wide stance. At rest, facing on, the pick hangs by its neck from the near hand; facing away, it rests on the shoulder. Finds that pop up get a 12×12 pixel icon (`DTWSprites.item`). Exhibits are bone shapes on a 48×28 grid, one per creature indexed `w*3 + c`, split into its five pieces; missing pieces draw as a dotted ghost.
- `js/render.js` is the isometric renderer (`DTWRender`): cube sprites built from 16×16 pixel textures drawn in code, the cut-away pit, the ladder, wall torches, the plaza props placed from the map (sorted by footprint in `depthSort`), and the people on the plaza. Hired helper diggers are drawn where the logic puts them (`run.crew`), easing between cells, and resting by their hut on the plaza. Visitors are visual only and use `Math.random`, never the game's rng. With WebGL2 it draws the scene into an offscreen canvas and glowing things (ores, torches, goo, the relic, the headlamp) into a half-size emissive canvas, collects point lights, and hands all three to `DTWPost`; without WebGL2 it draws straight to `#cv` in plain 2D. Floating text and stun stars go on the `#ov` overlay canvas so the blur never touches them. Lighting and grading numbers sit in `GRADE` and `lamp()` in `render.js`.
- `js/audio.js` is the WebAudio sound effects and per-world music (`DTWAudio`).
- `js/ui.js` holds state, saving, the HUD, surface menus, input and the main loop, and sets `window.__game`.
- `tests/logic.test.mjs`, `tests/materials.test.mjs` and `tests/hub.test.mjs` run `js/logic.js` in a Node `vm` context.

## Grid and controls

- Cells are indexed `(y*N + z)*N + x` with `N = 7`; use `DTW.idx(x, y, z)`. `y` is depth: layer 0 is open air, the relic layer is at `DEPTH` (50) and bedrock is below it.
- The miner works the topmost layer that still has diggable blocks (`DTW.topLayer(site)`); open cells (air, bubbles, craters, jungle rooms) need no clearing. The layer below opens only when that layer is empty: then the miner digs the block underfoot, or drops through an open cell, and stops in the first layer that still has blocks. The relic layer works the same way; its first block gives the relic, and later ones are treasure.
- Each world has `CREATURES` (3) skeletons of `PIECES` (5); a fossil's index is `c*5 + piece` and `museum[w].pieces` has 15 counts. Each creature is buried twice over in its own band of depth (`BAND`). The two largest pieces of each creature (`BIG_FORMS`, one string per world, sized from the exhibit shapes in `sprites.js`) are big fossils: one piece spanning two cells in a row, three in a row or a 2×2 square of one layer, turned either way. Each cell is a fossil block with its own hp, and its value packs the piece and its place in the footprint (`idx + 16 * part`, `PARTS`), so saves need nothing extra; `DTW.fossilAt(g, i)` returns the piece, footprint and the cells still in the ground. Breaking a cell gives nothing (a `bigBone` event) until the last one breaks; that drops the one bone. `render.js` (`bigFossil`) sets the museum piece (`DTWSprites.pieceCanvas`) into the top faces, and the side faces along its length, of the cells it spans. Every world has treasure chests (`CHEST_EVERY`), the first in layer 2; a chest holds a missing piece of the creature buried at that depth (`CHEST_FOSSIL`) or coins. Chests are locked: when the miner reaches one, the dig pauses on a puzzle (`run.lock`, the `#lock` panel in `ui.js`), one of seven kinds (`LOCK_KINDS`) (`run.lock.kind`), never the same kind twice in a row: `pins`, a lock; `pairs`, a memory game of face-down cards (`PAIRS` pairs per world, two flipped at a time, a miss turns back after `PAIR_MISS` s); `simon`, four gems that light up in a sequence (`SIMON_LEN` per world) to tap back in order, a wrong tap replaying it. `slide`, sliding tiles (`SLIDE_N` 3×3 then 4×4, scrambled by `SLIDE_K` random moves; `L.tiles`, 0 the blank); `pipes`, a `PIPE_N` board of pipe tiles turned a quarter turn per tap until water runs from the left inlet to the right outlet (`pipeSides`, `pipeWet`, `pipeDone`); `odd`, `ODD_ROUNDS` rounds of `ODD_CARDS` look-alike cards with one odd one out (`ODD_SETS`, a miss waits `ODD_WAIT` s); `path`, `PATH_N` numbered gems on a `PATH_GRID` square tapped in order, the numbers hidden after `PATH_PREVIEW` s from the third world on (a miss shows them for `PATH_RECALL` s and restarts). All of them go through `DTW.lockTap(s, k)`, and their timers run in `stepDig` while the dig waits; `DTW._lockKind(kind)` forces a kind in tests. A pin lock's pins (`LOCK_PINS` per world, 4 to 6; `LOCK_LEVELS` 4 heights) must match the key's teeth; a tap (`DTW.lockTap`, or number keys) raises a pin one step, wrapping round, and its linked neighbours with it (`LOCK_LINK`, `run.lock.link`): in the first world only the pin to its right, so it is solved left to right; after that the pins on both sides, so the taps must be planned (the tests solve locks by search). Solving any puzzle gives the contents plus a bonus gem (`LOCK_GEM`); the always-available 🔨 Smash button (`DTW.lockSmash`, which must be held for 1 s, `SMASH_HOLD` in `ui.js`, so a stray tap never breaks a chest) gives only `SMASH_COINS` and never a fossil. Helpers leave chests for the miner, and going home closes an open lock.
- The miner carries one new bone at a time (`s.carry`, saved), drawn on its back. While it carries one (or one lies uncollected), fossil blocks stay in the ground, so a layer with a fossil left is not finished. It hands the bone in by standing beside that creature's plinth or on the museum desk pad `M` (a `delivered` event); while a bone is carried a thin gold ring marks the spot by the plinth, a line of chevrons on the floor leads there along the walking path, and a small dark tag over the ring names the skeleton (`handInFloor`, `handInTop` in `render.js`). A copy of a bone already on show goes in the bag and sells.
- Materials: `MATS` (tiers 0–4) are counted in `s.mats`. Each world's `ores` table has the five materials (index = tier, `mat` field) and the themed gem at index 5 (`GEM`, sells for coins); `ORE_WEIGHT` gives each world's ore mix. `bank` adds materials to `s.mats` (the `banked` event carries `mats`); gems, treasure and spare bones still become coins, and the museum pays coins. `upgradeCost` and `hireCost` return `{ coins, mats }` (check with `DTW.canAfford`): the old coin curve's value is split 40% coins, 30% the level's top tier and the rest in lower tiers, capped by `LOW_CAP`, so high levels still need coal and copper in bulk; level 0 costs materials only. `buyUpgrade` and `hire` never convert. The shop exchanges: `sellMat` pays `MAT_PRICE[t]`, `buyMat` costs `BUY_SPREAD` times that plus `BUY_RAMP` per unit bought since the last trip home (`s.matBought`, reset by `bank`).
- Hired helpers really dig: each takes its own block of the miner's layer (away from the miner's target and at least two cells off when it can), and its finds go into the bag. A trap a helper breaks dazes that helper, not the miner. The player may hire `DIGGERS_FIRST` (2) helpers plus one per world unlocked after the first (`DTW.maxDiggers`), and only 2 + world index of them dig in a world. Helpers have their own tools, bought at the hut once one is hired (`CREW_UPGRADES`, kept in `s.upgrades` as `crewDrill` and `crewSpeed`): `crewDamage` and `crewRate` (`HELPER_RATE` scaled like the miner's speed), independent of the miner's pick.
- Museum visitors' coins (`museumIncome`) pile up in `s.pile` (saved) instead of going to the purse; walking onto any museum floor cell or the desk pad collects all whole coins in it (a `museumCash` event). The heap is drawn just inside the museum door (`coinPile` in `render.js`), growing with the sum, and the plaza HUD shows the amount waiting.
- Auto-mining can be switched off (`DTW.setAuto`, the 🤖 Auto / ✋ Me button, Q or Tab; saved as `s.auto`). Off, the miner moves and digs only when told; a cleared layer still opens the next.
- Left alone with auto-mining on, the miner walks the shortest path over open cells of its layer to the nearest block and digs it, both at `AUTO_PACE` (half) of its steered pace, so steering pays. Steered it walks `WALK` cells per second and swings at full rate. Hired diggers' share of the hit rate is never slowed.
- `DTW.steer(s, dir)` picks the neighbouring block in that direction as the next one to dig, or walks onto it if it is open, and pauses auto-walk for `MANUAL_HOLD` (1.5 s) after the last steer; a steered block is dug till it breaks, so the miner stays where the player put it. A press moves or digs one cell; holding longer than 0.35 s (`REPEAT` in `ui.js`) keeps steering, one cell at a time at `WALK` pace. `DTW.digAt(s, x, z)` (a tap on a block of the layer) walks next to that block and digs it, and on an open cell of the layer walks onto it at full pace (`run.dest`), holding auto-walk off for `MANUAL_HOLD`; a tap while standing still hits the block the miner faces (`run.face`). It takes `'n'`, `'s'`, `'e'` or `'w'`; `-1` and `+1` are aliases for `'w'` and `'e'`. East is +x and south is +z. `run.steer` holds the key or `0`.
- On screen +x runs down-right and +z runs down-left, so the directions are diagonals: ↗ north, ↘ east, ↙ south, ↖ west. In the pit the joystick (outside a small dead zone the screen quadrant of the drag is held), the arrow keys (Up north, Right east, Down south, Left west) and WASD the same way all follow this. Space or a tap on the pit away from a block digs faster, H goes home, Enter digs from the surface, M mutes.
- Finds from broken blocks (ore, treasure, chest contents, fossils; not the relic, which goes straight to the bag) drop as `run.loot` on the floor of the cell where the block was, whoever broke it (miner or helper). The miner picks loot up when it is in the same layer and within `pickupReach(magnet)` cells (`PICKUP_BASE` 0.75 plus `PICKUP_PER_MAGNET` 1 per magnet level, measured on the floor plane). With a magnet, loot inside the reach slides toward the miner (`MAG_SPEED`) and is collected on arrival. The bag, its weight and the HUD count only what has been collected. A new bone goes into `s.carry` only when hands are free; otherwise it stays on the floor, and fossil blocks stay in the ground while a bone is carried or lying uncollected (`handsFull`). Auto-mining goes for whichever is nearer, the next loot or the next block (a tie goes to the loot), a cleared layer is swept (even with auto-mining off) before the miner digs down, and `goHome` puts any loot left in the pit into the bag. Moon loot bounces over the open cells of its layer, then settles; it no longer drifts to the miner. A faint ring at the miner's feet shows the reach once the magnet is upgraded.
- The plaza map is `HUB.rows` in `js/logic.js`, one character per cell from `(HUB.x0, HUB.z0)`, in the same x, z frame as the pit (its key is in the comment above it). The pads `L S H W M` open the dig, shop, helpers, worlds and museum menus. The miner starts on the dig pad at the top of the ladder and returns there after every dig; its plaza position is not saved.
- On the plaza, held arrow keys and WASD walk that way on screen (Up is up the screen; `ui.js` turns each key into two compass directions), the held joystick walks the nearest of eight screen directions, all at `HUB_SPEED`, and a tap walks to that spot by the shortest path (holding brings up the joystick, which cancels that walk). The ⛏ Dig! button and Enter start digging from anywhere.
- Going home and going back down are drawn as the miner climbing a ladder on the back-left pit wall (the x = 0 face at z = 3), which runs from the rim to the current layer. The logic keeps its `elevT` timers (at most 2 s down and 3 s up), so long climbs speed up. Steering west from the cell beside the ladder (x = 0, z = 3; up-left on screen) climbs home, and on the plaza pushing mostly east (down-right on screen) from the dig pad climbs down.

## Commands

- Run the tests with `node --test tests/*.test.mjs`. The playthrough test uses a simple bot (it steers toward the nearest block, taps, and goes home when the bag is full or every 2.5 minutes) to check that every world takes 8–20 minutes and that full bags send it home, and prints the minutes per world.
- Play the game by serving the folder with `python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1` and opening `http://localhost:8765/`.
- URL flags:
  - `?dev=1` gives a billion coins and unlocks every world, and does not save.
  - `?world=N` starts in world N, counting from 0.
  - `?fresh=1` starts a new game without loading or overwriting the save.
  - `?nosplash=1` skips the title screen (audio starts on the first tap).
  - `?seed=N` sets the random seed for a new game.
  - `?hd=0` turns off the HD-2D post-process and draws in plain 2D (the WebGL2 fallback). The console logs which renderer is running.
- In the browser console, `window.__game` exposes `s` (the state), `D` (the logic) and `step(seconds)`, which advances the game frame by frame even when the tab is throttled and draws once at the end.
- Test and dev hooks in the logic: `DTW._teleport(s, x, y, z)`, `DTW._setTile(s, x, y, z, tile, value)`, `DTW._setFossil(s, x0, y, z0, idx, w, d)` (a big fossil) and `DTW._clearLayer(s, y)`.

## Conventions

- Game rules and numbers live in `js/logic.js` and are tested first. Rendering sits on top and is not tested.
- To change balance, edit `hp`, `oreMult` and `exhibit.income` in `WORLDS`, `ORE_P`, `WALK`, `AUTO_PACE`, `HELPER_RATE`, `BAND`, `CHEST_EVERY`, the cost tables in `UPGRADES` and `WORKERS`, or `ORE_WEIGHT`, `MAT_PRICE`, `BUY_SPREAD`, `BUY_RAMP` and `LOW_CAP`. Then rerun the tests to check the per-world times.
- Art is original and drawn in code. Do not copy names, sprites or layouts from other digging games.
- Never call `alert`, `confirm` or `prompt`. The two resets (Restart world, Start over) open an inline box and act only once yes is typed in it; while that box is open the panel is not redrawn, and game keys ignore typing in it.
- Saves go to `localStorage` under the key `dig-through-worlds-v1`, and every access is wrapped in try/catch. The save format is version 4 (adds `mats`). Older saves load with no materials, and an old-format ore in a saved bag becomes coins at its old value (`OLD_ORE_VALUE`). A version 2 save's single skeleton per world becomes that world's middle creature (pieces and buried fossils shift by 5). A version 1 save from the old flat 11-wide world keeps coins, upgrades, museum and unlocks, and its dig sites are regenerated.

## Agent workflow

- Fanning independent work out to parallel subagents (for example one for the logic and tests, one for rendering, one for CSS and controls) is pre-approved here; do not ask first (standing rule, 2026-11-21). Keep to the model tiers and concurrency limits in the global instructions, give each agent its own files so edits do not collide, and review and test the merged result before committing.
